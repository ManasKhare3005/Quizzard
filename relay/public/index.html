The pitch

Two and a half minutes. The whole idea.

The problem

Every study group chat dies the same way.

"We should study together." Then memes, for six days, until someone types "wait the exam is TOMORROW??"

😴

Studying alone is a chore

Rereading notes feels productive and mostly isn't.

📅

Group calls never happen

Five schedules, zero calendar invites.

🫥

Nobody keeps anyone honest

Skipping is free when nobody's counting.

How a round goes

One message starts it. Cubbie runs the rest.

1

Type @test

Cubbie pulls your upcoming exams from your Canvas. Pick a topic, vote on a time, it's locked.

2

Check in

30 minutes out, reply ready or excused. At 10 minutes, the group hears who's missing. No check-in means a DQ.

3

Answer privately

Questions arrive 1:1, one at a time. Answers are final and sealed. Nobody copies.

4

Learn the miss

Results drop for everyone at once, with the most-missed question, its answer, the lecture slide, and the winner teaches it.

Points & the monthly scorecard

Friendly competition, with receipts.

Points add up all month. On the last day, Cubbie posts the scorecard and the treat is settled, with a budget cap so it stays fun.

Why it works

It's a game. It's also how memory works.

1

Retrieval practice

Quizzing yourself is one of the best-proven ways to remember, far better than rereading notes.

2

Teach it back

The winner explains the toughest question. Explaining locks it in, for them and everyone listening.

3

Accountability

When your friends notice you skipped, you show up. Small, social, real stakes.

Under the hood
